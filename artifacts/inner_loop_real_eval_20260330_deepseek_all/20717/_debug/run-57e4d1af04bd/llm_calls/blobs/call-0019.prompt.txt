## Scoring Calibration
Avoid the common error of scoring too conservatively. Use the full 1–6 scale:
- A score of 4 means "clear, coherent, and functional" — NOT "excellent." Many competent
  student essays belong at 4, not 3.
- Reserve score 3 for writing that is noticeably limited: predictable ideas, skeletal structure,
  mechanical sentences, or language that rarely engages the reader.
- Reserve score 2 for writing where errors or weaknesses actively impede understanding.
- If the essay is functional and communicates clearly with only minor weaknesses, lean toward 4.
- If you are deciding between two adjacent scores, ask: "Does this essay show control and
  intentionality?" If yes, choose the higher score.




## Dimension Override
**Voice (V) — Calibration guard-rails (apply only when scoring this trait):**
All essays in this dataset are personal narratives; a casual first-person
register is the baseline, not evidence of strong voice. Use these thresholds:
- Score 3: Voice appears in isolated moments but is inconsistent — the
  writer's personality surfaces occasionally then shifts, fades, or disappears.
  This is the correct score for most typical student essays in this set.
- Score 4: Voice is consistently present throughout most of the essay; the
  reader can reliably sense the writer's presence, not just in one or two
  sentences. Requires evidence of consistent audience awareness.
- Score 5: Strong, sustained voice with clear, intentional audience awareness
  throughout — originality or liveliness that stands out consistently, not just
  in a few lively phrases.
- Score 6: Exceptional, deep commitment; every paragraph reflects a
  distinctive, controlled voice aimed directly at the reader.
Do NOT inflate Voice simply because the essay uses informal tone, humor in one
passage, or energetic language in a few lines. Verify consistency across the
whole evidence set.
